# M6: Step 6, Bonus Feature: In-app Notifications

## What I built
- **Notifications table** in `cems/db.py`, added to `TABLES_CHILD_FIRST` so demo reset clears it. Recipients are matched case-insensitively (`COLLATE NOCASE`), and there's an index on (recipient, is_read).
- **`cems/notifications.py`**: `notify`, `list_for`, `unread_count`, `mark_read`, `mark_all_read`, and the shared `REVIEWER_INBOX`. Also a `Notification` model.
- **Hooks in `cems/services.py`**:
  - submit → reviewers;
  - approve or reject (with the reason) → organiser;
  - cancel → the other party: the organiser if the Events Office cancelled, otherwise the reviewers.
- **`ui/inbox.py`**: an Inbox tab on the Requester and Reviewer pages, with the unread count in the tab label, NEW badges and *Mark all as read*.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_notifications.py` (14) and `tests/test_app_inbox.py` (4). The suite now has 168 tests.
- **Docs:** README "Bonus feature" section; assumptions A21–A23.

## Why this bonus
The brief names *"limited visibility into request status"* and *"delays in reviewing and confirming events"* as pain points. Telling reviewers when a request arrives cuts review delay. Telling organisers the outcome, including the rejection reason, gives them visibility without chasing the office.

## Business rule I own
**A notification exists if and only if its change happened (A23).** `notify` runs inside the service's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction. A refused submit, a refused approval or an unauthorised cancel sends nothing. If the notification insert fails, the status change itself is rolled back.

## Assumption / trade-off
- **A21, one shared reviewer inbox:** there are no user accounts, so reviewers are addressed by role. The trade-off is that reading the inbox marks it read for every reviewer.
- **A22, in-app only:** there is no email, because the brief rules out external services. Email or SMS would be the next step.

## How I validated it
- **Validated the previous step:** pulled Step 5, ran `pytest -q` (151 passed), and approved, rejected and blocked in the browser.
- **Store tests:** newest-first ordering, case-insensitive recipients, per-recipient unread counts, and marking read.
- **Hook tests:** exact message text per transition; who is and isn't notified on each cancel path; the full lifecycle produces 2 reviewer and 1 organiser notifications.
- **Failure tests:**
  - failed or refused actions send nothing;
  - a broken `notify` rolls back the approval itself;
  - reset clears notifications.
- **UI tests:**
  - the reviewer inbox shows *Inbox (1 new)*;
  - approving on the Reviewer page, then switching to Requester, shows the approval in the inbox;
  - *Mark all as read* clears the count.
- **Result:** `pytest -q` → 168 passed.

## AI suggestion I corrected
The AI first proposed committing each notification separately after the status change ("so a notification failure can't block an approval"). That allows a notification for a change that was rolled back, or a change with no notification. I kept `notify` inside the service transaction and added `test_notification_rolls_back_with_its_transition` to lock that in.
